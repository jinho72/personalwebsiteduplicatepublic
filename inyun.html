Overview

INYUN:MEDIATED is an exploration into how digitally mediated physical space impacts human emotion regulation. The aim of this project is to create a space for vulnerability that provides a space of retreat, rehabilitation, and vulnerability for people in metropolis.

Technologies

  • HTML CSS JS / Three.Js
  • Three.js & WebXR
  • Supabase: Database
  • Railway: Deployment
  • React: Frontend Framework
  • Vercel: Frontend Deployment

Process
01
Research

Surveying existing AR character frameworks and identifying gaps in environmental responsiveness. Establishing theoretical grounding in embodied cognition and spatial presence theory.

02
Prototype

Rapid-cycle prototyping using WebXR. Testing five distinct behavioral models across varied physical environments — domestic, urban, institutional — with structured observation sessions.

03
Refine

User feedback sessions across three cohorts. Iterating on creature animation rigs, collision response, and the emotional legibility of environmental reactions under varying light conditions.

04
Deploy

Public installation across four Manhattan locations over two weeks. Simultaneous release of open-source toolkit for independent developers working in spatial computing.

Key Findings
F.01
Environmental Texture
Creatures that responded to surface material — recoiling from concrete, moving differently on grass — were rated significantly more "alive" by participants than those using uniform behavioral rules.
F.02
Peripheral Presence
The most compelling moments occurred when creatures appeared in peripheral vision rather than center frame. Designing for the edge of attention proved more effective than designing for direct gaze.
F.03
Scale Discontinuity
Unexpected scale transitions — creatures suddenly shrinking or growing in response to ambient sound — created the strongest emotional responses. Consistency of scale was counterproductive.
F.04
The Last Crystal Problem
Users consistently attempted to collect or interact with glowing volumetric elements, regardless of instruction. Future iterations should either lean into this or actively disrupt the expectation.
